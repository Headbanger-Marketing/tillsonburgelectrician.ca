Before work begins

Sort repair from replacement before you ask for quotes.

Later finishing work can put wiring out of sight. A bungalow or split-level with a finished basement may have cables running through ceilings and walls closed in after the original wiring, and its panel may sit inside a finished room or behind storage.

Write down which items you think need repair, which need replacement and which can stay as they are, with the reason for each. A contractor can then confirm or correct each reason in the written scope instead of defaulting to replacement.

What records help decide between repairing and replacing?

Gather earlier ESA paperwork, invoices, product sheets and any inspection report, plus photos of labels you can see without opening anything. These show what was done before, which helps the contractor separate worn items from sound ones.

Should a working outlet be replaced if it looks old?

Age and appearance alone do not settle it. A contractor can assess whether the device and its circuit are sound, then say in writing whether leaving it, repairing it or replacing it fits the evidence.

How do I compare quotes when one repairs and one replaces?

Ask each contractor to name the fault or condition behind the proposal and to list what is included. Quotes that cover different work cannot be compared line by line until both describe the same scope.

Who has to approve work that opens a finished ceiling or wall?

The person who owns the property decides, and a condominium corporation or landlord may also need to agree where one is involved. Ask the contractor to state in writing who restores the finish and who pays for it.

Once I submit my details, who decides what happens next?

Any provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you deal with settles the next steps directly with you. It must separately confirm who it is and its licence, agree the job and its cost, and set the dates.
