EV charger installation in Tillsonburg.

An EV charger at a Tillsonburg house can look simple when the garage is attached and near the panel, but a short route does not show whether the existing outlet, circuit or supply suits charging. The assessment settles what can be kept.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Tillsonburg property

Photograph the parking position, the garage wall nearest the panel and any existing outlet where the vehicle could plug in. Note what else runs in that garage, such as a freezer, a workbench or a door opener.

Ask whether any existing garage circuit or outlet is suitable to keep for charging, or whether a dedicated circuit is needed, and why. Ask what the route from the panel crosses, including finished walls or a basement ceiling.

What should the assessment establish?

Whether the existing circuit, outlet and supply in a Tillsonburg garage close to the panel can be kept for charging, or a new dedicated circuit is needed, is for the contractor to judge. A short cable route can reduce the work, yet it shows nothing about capacity for the added load; the contractor gives the load estimate in plain terms and names the approved equipment and any outdoor-rated parts in the scope. The route past any finished surface and who repairs it are written down too. As for ESA, the scope says which party files the notification.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is an existing garage outlet enough to charge an electric vehicle?

It may not be. An outlet that suits a freezer or a tool is not automatically suitable for hours of vehicle charging, so ask the contractor to assess that circuit and say in writing whether it can stay.

Should the charger be chosen before the circuit is assessed?

Not necessarily, but a shortlist helps. Product sheets let the contractor compare each unit's needs with the circuit, and a firm choice made before the assessment may have to be changed if the circuit cannot support it.

Useful project details

  • Vehicle and charger model if chosen
  • Parking position and preferred equipment location
  • Indoor or outdoor conditions
  • Existing supply records if available
  • Other major loads and future charging needs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
