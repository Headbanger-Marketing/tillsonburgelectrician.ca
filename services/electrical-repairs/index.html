A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Tillsonburg property

Write down what stopped working, what you noticed first and what had been running when the power came back. Note any warm surface, smell, buzzing or flicker without touching the equipment or opening any cover.

Ask whether the contractor can tell an item that wore out from one that was affected by the interruption, and what evidence supports each view. Ask which items could be left as they are for now.

What should the assessment establish?

A list of parts to replace, with no word on what a power interruption actually touched, leaves a Tillsonburg owner unable to see why a fault first noticed afterwards needs that work. What is affected is established before anything is replaced. Damage from the interruption, an older weakness the event exposed and a device that had simply worn out are kept apart. Each affected item carries the contractor's chosen response, whether repair, replacement or no action, with the evidence that supports it. The circuits and devices examined are named in writing, and so are the limits of what could be seen.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Should I keep resetting a device that stopped after an interruption?

No. Repeated resetting can hide a pattern the contractor needs to see. Note how often it stops and what was running, then leave the device alone and describe the history when you send the enquiry.

Is everything that stopped working after an interruption damaged?

Not necessarily. Some items may only be disturbed, some may be worn and some may be unaffected. Ask the contractor which items were checked and what each check showed, so the list of repairs matches the evidence.
