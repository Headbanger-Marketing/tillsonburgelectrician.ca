An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your Tillsonburg property

Find the rating label on the new and the old appliance, note where each plug or connection sits and photograph the receptacle and the wall around it. Write down what else shares that laundry area.

Ask whether the existing receptacle and circuit can be kept for the new equipment, and what in the product sheet led to that answer. Ask what changes if the laundry moves to another room.

What should the assessment establish?

Swapping laundry equipment at a Tillsonburg home means the assessment compares the new appliance's requirements with the existing receptacle and circuit. Where the two match, the report says so and the arrangement is left alone, with no work added; where they do not, it explains what must change. In that case a dedicated circuit may prove necessary or unnecessary; the report gives the answer either way. The scope names the party filing the ESA notification and the one restoring the surfaces. In a basement laundry, it also describes how any new cable passes through finished walls or ceilings.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a new washer or dryer use the receptacle the old one used?

Sometimes, but it is not safe to assume. Compare the rating labels, then ask the contractor to confirm whether the receptacle and circuit suit the new appliance before it is delivered.

What if the old laundry receptacle looks worn but still works?

How a receptacle looks is not proof of a fault. A receptacle that is only scuffed may need no work, while one that is warm, discoloured or buzzing should be left alone, unused, until a contractor has assessed it.
