Panel and service upgrades in Tillsonburg.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Tillsonburg property

Sketch which rooms each unit will use and where the panel sits in relation to them. Photograph the panel door, its labels and the meter position from outside, with every cover left closed.

Ask the contractor whether the equipment already in place can serve one unit unchanged while the other gets new equipment, and why. Ask who deals with the utility, the municipality and ESA, and in what order.

What should the assessment establish?

A house is being divided into two living units, and the assessment settles whether its current panel and incoming supply can serve both or each unit needs its own. Where the evidence shows the existing panel is sound, it is kept rather than swapped by default. Access matters when that panel sits in a finished basement, so the scope marks where each set of equipment will be and how every occupant of this Tillsonburg house reaches it. The contractor names in writing who applies to the utility, the municipality and ESA, and the record lists any finished surfaces to be opened.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can my existing panel keep serving one unit once the house has two?

It may, if the contractor finds it sound for the loads it would carry. That is a judgement made on site, so ask for the reasoning in writing whichever way it goes.

Does a second living unit always need a new meter and service?

Not always, and the site cannot say which applies to your property. Whether the supply is shared or separate depends on the assessment, the utility's requirements and local rules, so ask the contractor to explain the reasoning.
