A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Tillsonburg property

Collect any earlier ESA paperwork, invoices for past electrical work and any seller disclosure or home inspection report. Note which areas, such as a finished basement, the contractor can reach without disturbing walls.

Ask what the assessment will cover, whether any testing is included and what the written result will say about items that cannot be reached. Ask how findings will be sorted into repair now, replace later or leave alone.

What should the assessment establish?

Which areas does the review cover, and which equipment does it leave out? With a Tillsonburg sale ahead, the report names its purpose alongside the areas covered and the equipment excluded. Does a finding bear on safety, or is it only cosmetic or dated? Safety items sit apart, and each finding is marked for repair, replacement or leaving alone, with the observation behind it. What stays hidden from view? Concealed wiring and unreachable equipment narrow what the report concludes, as it admits, and its wording is plain enough to hand to a buyer or an agent.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Do I have to replace everything a pre-sale review finds?

No. A finding can lead to repair, replacement or no action at all, and the contractor should say which and why. What you disclose to a buyer is a separate matter for a professional adviser.

Is a pre-sale electrical review the same as a buyer's home inspection?

No. A home inspection is a broad visual review of the whole house, while an electrical assessment is a defined scope agreed with a contractor. They can reach different conclusions, so keep both reports.
